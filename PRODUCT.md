# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Stitch Studio serves knitters, crocheters, chart designers, and pattern authors who need a practical charting tool that opens immediately in a browser. They may be working at a desktop desk, on a tablet beside yarn and notes, or on a phone while checking a pattern in progress. Their job is to make, revise, export, and follow stitch charts without losing work or getting trapped in a paid, account-bound, device-bound system.

## Product Purpose

Stitch Studio is a free, install-free, account-free chart designer for knit and crochet work. It supports square and hex grids, crochet and knitting stitch vocabularies, gauge-aware cells, no-stitch placeholders, wrong-side row marking, follow mode, granny square stamping, autosave, portable project files, and print/export workflows. Success means a maker can create a real pattern chart, recover it safely, export it legibly, and understand the interface without reading a manual first.

## Brand Personality

Practical, calm, craft-literate. The product should feel like a trustworthy studio table: organized, tactile, and specific to textile work. It can be warm and a little charming, but the interface should stay out of the maker's hands once they start drawing.

## Anti-references

Do not look like a generic SaaS dashboard, a marketing landing page, a decorative AI-generated craft moodboard, or a subscription-gated design tool. Avoid lock-in cues, cloudy collaboration chrome, dark neon maker aesthetics, gratuitous gradients, glass effects, and novelty controls where standard buttons, tabs, toggles, and fields would be clearer. Do not bury primary work behind modals or make mobile users hunt through hidden horizontal overflow.

## Design Principles

1. The canvas is the workspace. Controls should support drawing, reading, and exporting charts without crowding the chart more than necessary.
2. Familiar controls beat clever controls. Use standard buttons, grouped toggles, labels, file actions, and progressive disclosure so makers can move quickly.
3. No lock-in, no lost work. Save, autosave, import, export, and print workflows need clear names and defensive feedback.
4. Craft semantics matter. Text export, symbols, terminology, repeats, no-stitch cells, gauge, and cables should match how real patterns are written.
5. Tablet use is first-class. Touch, stylus, panel collapse, and mobile headers must be designed as primary workflows, not reduced desktop leftovers.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for controls and text. Support keyboard canvas editing, visible focus states, reduced motion, high-contrast or forced-colors modes, color-blind-safe labeling, and robust text wrapping. Treat motor precision as a core accessibility concern because dense grids, stylus input, trackpads, and small touch screens are central to the product.
